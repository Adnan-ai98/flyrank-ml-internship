Ranking Content Pages for Refresh Review

A public-safe search-intelligence study comparing supervised machine learning with a transparent rules-based baseline using anonymized March 2026 content-performance data.

Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring · Validation: Client holdout · Use: Human decision support

Abstract

This study asks whether a supervised machine-learning model can improve the ranking of content pages for refresh review compared with a transparent rules-based baseline. Anonymized FlyRank internship data from March 2026 was used to engineer observable search and engagement signals and evaluate models with a client-holdout design. Random Forest achieved 0.740 Precision@50 compared with 0.240 for the baseline. Its ranking had a Spearman correlation of 0.8638 with the baseline, with 10 of the top 20 pages overlapping. These are directional decision-support results, not proof that refreshing content causes traffic growth or that the model represents Google's ranking algorithm.

1. Introduction / Problem statement

Content teams have more pages to investigate than they can review immediately. The decision supported here is: which pages should a reviewer investigate first? The system produces a ranked review queue rather than automatically publishing content changes.

2. Data

The analysis uses the anonymized FlyRank ML Internship warehouse release flyrank_pseudonymized_warehouse_release_v20260703 and focuses on March 2026. Signals include GSC impressions, clicks, average position, derived CTR, GA4 sessions, and engaged sessions. Client names, domains, URLs, private queries, raw exports, credentials, and private content are excluded.

3. Methodology

Label

is_declining_label is an observed decline proxy based on search-impression movement between the first and second halves of March 2026. It is not a causal refresh label.

Baseline

Baseline review rule: at least 500 impressions, average position 4–20, and CTR below 2%. Score: impressions × (0.02 − CTR). Reason code: high_volume_low_ctr_for_position.

Validation & leakage

Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest were evaluated using a client-holdout split. Leakage checks excluded the label, label-derived fields, future-window measurements, and product decision flags from model inputs.

5. Limitations & honest framing

  • The target is an observed decline proxy, not a causal refresh outcome.
  • CTR is affected by query mix, SERP features, position, seasonality, and other factors.
  • The model ranks candidates for human review; it does not decide what to change.
  • The evaluation is tied to this anonymized dataset and validation design.
  • No result proves Google's ranking algorithm or causal traffic impact.

6. Ranked recommendations

  1. Review high-confidence opportunities first: prioritize strong exposure plus clear CTR or decline-related weakness.
  2. Investigate before changing: check query mix, SERP features, seasonality, consolidation, and measurement availability.
  3. Monitor lower-confidence predictions: use weaker signals for monitoring rather than automatic action.

Operational rule: Review first → investigate second → change third.

9. Closing takeaway

The useful outcome is a more defensible way to spend limited review time: combine a transparent baseline with a validated learned ranking, retain reason codes, and keep the final content decision with a human reviewer.
